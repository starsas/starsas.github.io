About

I am a third-year M.Eng. student at Sun Yat-sen University, advised by Prof. Xiaojun Tan. I received my B.Eng. in Intelligence Science and Technology from the same school in 2024.

My research asks how we can generate the driving scenes that real data rarely contains, such as unusual vehicle poses, distant objects, and heavy snow or fog, while keeping their 3D labels valid, and use them to find and fix the failures of 3D perception models. I work across 3D reconstruction from real multi-sensor data (Gaussian splatting), diffusion-based generation and editing, and LLM agents for scene editing and multi-vehicle decision-making.

I am applying for Ph.D. programs starting Fall 2027. Feel free to reach out by email.

Research Interests

  • Safety-critical scenario generation: controllable synthesis of long-tail object states and adverse weather, with labels that stay geometrically valid.
  • Reliable 3D perception: stress-testing and improving detectors on rare, out-of-distribution, and adverse-condition inputs.
  • Editable traffic digital twins: metric-consistent driving environments reconstructed from real multi-sensor data and edited through language.

Publications

RECAST: Reference-Guided Calibrated Augmentation of Object States for Monocular 3D Detection

Rourou Chen, Xiangyi Qin, Xiaojun Tan*
IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA-L), under review  ·  *corresponding author

Monocular 3D detectors fail on rare object states such as unusual orientations and distant vehicles. RECAST samples these tail states on purpose and generates them with a reference-guided diffusion model: a 2D exemplar of the vehicle plus a target 3D box, with cuboid-face conditioning for pose control and no CAD assets. A detector-in-the-loop step then checks each generated vehicle and re-fits its 3D label. On nuScenes, 22.9K synthetic instances improve FCOS3D by +1.2 mAP and +1.1 Rare-5 AP over a real-replay baseline, and the detector's errors on synthetic instances follow the same trends as its failures on real data.

GeoWeatherEdit: Label-Preserving Adverse-Weather Scenario Generation

May 2026 – present · in progress

Weather editing that changes the whole image tends to distort vehicles and road structure, which breaks their labels. GeoWeatherEdit splits the problem in two. First, it composites object instances in 3D: SAM3D-reconstructed assets are placed only where road region, LiDAR ground height, ground contact, BEV occupancy, and camera visibility all check out. Then a training-free, one-step diffusion editor (ChordEdit) adds snow, rain, or fog. In a pilot on snow, rain, and fog against WeatherEdit, DriveGEN, and standard ChordEdit, it gave the highest background-structure preservation (edge F1 0.531) and tied for the highest CLIP-direction score (0.162). Downstream 3D detection experiments are underway.

Sensor-to-Twin: Editable Traffic Digital Twins from Real Multi-Sensor Data

Sep. 2024 – present · Shenzhen Key Research Project

Turning real campus captures into driving scenes that can be edited. I reconstructed self-collected scenes as Gaussian digital twins under three sensor setups: camera only (3DGS), vehicle cameras plus LiDAR (Street Gaussians), and ground plus UAV images (HorizonGS), and traced floating artifacts to a ~90 ms camera–LiDAR desynchronization. Because these reconstructions have no real-world scale, I recovered metric scale from lane geometry and inverse perspective mapping, so inserted vehicles respect lanes, collisions, and visibility. On top, I re-engineered an open-source LLM multi-agent editor to work across all three backends: a task planner splits each language instruction into edits (insert, remove, view, restore, modify), and specialized agents carry them out in a fixed order over a shared scene state, changing the viewpoint, inserting, removing, or moving vehicles, and adding rain, snow, or fog. Edits are demonstrated on our campus scenes and on public driving datasets.

LLM-Based Cooperative Decision-Making for Highway On-Ramp Merging

Jun. 2025 · Independent project, Multi-Agent Systems course

Connected vehicles take turns in a priority order, and each one runs an LLM agent that perceives its surroundings through tool calls, ranks candidate actions, and keeps the first one that passes traffic-rule and trajectory-conflict checks; its choice is then shared as an intent with the vehicles that decide next. In an 8-vehicle mixed-traffic scenario, it achieved a higher merge success rate than rule-based, virtual-queue, and game-theoretic baselines, with no potential conflicts, and every vehicle merged successfully at 25%, 50%, and 75% CAV penetration.
