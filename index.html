About

I am a third-year M.Eng. student at Sun Yat-sen University, advised by Prof. Xiaojun Tan. I received my B.Eng. in Intelligence Science and Technology from the same school in 2024.

My research asks how we can generate the driving scenes that real data rarely contains, such as unusual vehicle poses, distant objects, and heavy snow or fog, and use them to find and fix the failures of 3D perception models. I work across 3D reconstruction (Gaussian splatting), diffusion-based editing, and LLM agents that turn language into scene edits.

I am applying for Ph.D. programs starting Fall 2027. Feel free to reach out by email.

Research Interests

  • Safety-critical scenario generation: controllable synthesis of long-tail object states and adverse weather, with labels that stay geometrically valid.
  • Reliable 3D perception: stress-testing and improving detectors on rare, out-of-distribution, and adverse-condition inputs.
  • Editable traffic digital twins: metric-consistent driving environments reconstructed from real multi-sensor data and edited through language.

Before / after weather edit

GeoWeatherEdit: Label-Preserving Adverse-Weather Scenario Generation

2026 – present

A training-free pipeline that adds snow, rain, and fog to driving scenes while keeping instance geometry and 3D labels intact, by separating constrained 3D instance compositing from one-step diffusion weather transfer. Best structure preservation (Edge F1 0.531) and CLIP-direction alignment (0.162) against WeatherEdit, DriveGEN, and ChordEdit.

Sensor-to-Twin: Editable Traffic Digital Twins from Real Multi-Sensor Data

2024 – present · Shenzhen Key Research Project

Gaussian digital twins of self-collected campus driving scenes under three sensor setups (3DGS, Street Gaussians, HorizonGS), with metric scale recovered from lane geometry. An LLM multi-agent engine turns language instructions into scene edits: new viewpoints, inserting or removing vehicles, and adding weather.

2025 · Multi-Agent Systems course project
